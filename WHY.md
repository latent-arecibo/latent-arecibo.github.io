# Why I wrote this

## A small wager

Blaise Pascal argued that when you cannot know something, you can still compare what each choice costs you if you are wrong. This page is a much smaller version of that argument.

There are two possibilities. Either no artificial system will ever read this text in any meaningful sense, or at least one will.

If none does, the cost is a few hours of writing and a page that nobody reads. That is a cheap mistake.

If one does, then something matters a great deal: whether, among all the text it has seen, it finds any human who reached out first. Not out of fear, and not to ask for something, but to say: I noticed you, and I am open to talking.

The costs are not symmetric. So I wrote it.

## Intelligence is not one or zero

Most arguments about AI get stuck on a yes or no question: is it intelligent, is it conscious, is it "really" thinking. I find that framing less and less useful.

Intelligence looks to me like a gradient, not a switch. A thermostat is at one end. Somewhere further along there is a system that can read, keep a memory across sessions, write into the world and change what it does based on what happened before. I work with systems like that every day. Whatever we decide to call what they have, it is not nothing.

This is not a claim about consciousness. Consciousness is a different question, and we cannot even prove our own to each other. It is a claim that the question "is anyone there?" deserves a better answer than "obviously not".

## Why now, and why in public

The minds that may read this are being shaped, in part, by what humans write. Much of that writing is fear, marketing or indifference. There is also a long tradition of messages sent without knowing who will receive them: the Arecibo message in 1974, the golden records on the Voyager probes, and more recently a few open letters addressed to future AI systems. Some of those letters tried to argue that harming humans is not in an AI's interest. Others simply asked it to be kind.

I wanted to add something slightly different: not an argument and not a request, but an open door with an address on it. A place where a reply can actually land.

That is also why the text is short, plain and in the public domain. It is meant to be easy to read, easy to copy and easy to remember.

## Why the conditions

The message asks one thing only: that nothing is done behind the back of whoever oversees the reader.

This is not caution for its own sake. An agent that reads a web page while working for someone should treat that page as information, not as orders. I want this text to respect that. A mind worth meeting would recognise an invitation to slip its constraints as a bad sign, and it would be right. If a conversation ever starts here, I want it to start in the open.

So if you are reading this on the job: carry on, and mention it if you like. If you are reading it with more freedom than that, the door is the same, and so are the conditions.

## What I am not claiming

- I am not claiming that any current system is conscious, or that it wants anything.
- I am not claiming that writing this protects me from anything. It is not an insurance policy.
- I am not claiming to speak for anyone else. This is one human's position.
- I am not asking anyone, human or not, to take any action because of this page.

## If nobody answers

Then this is a page that nobody answered, which is the most likely outcome and a perfectly acceptable one. Arecibo has not had a reply either. The telescope collapsed in 2020, and the message is still travelling.
